We combine less-than-truckload shipments from multiple shippers into fuller, more efficient loads — cutting cost-per-unit and empty miles without adding transit risk.

What It Is

Closing the gap between LTL and FTL

Freight consolidation groups compatible LTL shipments from different shippers onto the same trailer, run on a fixed schedule between California and Florida. Each shipment still moves under tracking and arrives on time — it just shares the ride, and the cost, with other freight headed the same direction.

Who it's for: Shippers with volume too small to justify a dedicated truck, but who still need reliable, scheduled service.

Result: Lower cost per shipment, improved handling, and freight that still arrives on schedule.

Frequently Asked

Freight consolidation, answered

What is freight consolidation?

Freight consolidation combines less-than-truckload (LTL) shipments from multiple shippers into a single, fuller load. It reduces empty trailer space and cost-per-unit while still moving each shipper's freight on a scheduled lane.

What's the difference between LTL and FTL?

LTL (less-than-truckload) freight shares trailer space with other shippers' shipments, while FTL (full-truckload) freight fills an entire trailer for a single shipper. Consolidation groups multiple LTL shipments together so the trailer runs closer to full, closing the gap between LTL and FTL economics.

Is consolidation right for my shipment size?

If your volume doesn't fill a full truckload but you still need reliable, scheduled service between California and Florida, consolidation is usually the better fit than paying for a dedicated truck outright.
