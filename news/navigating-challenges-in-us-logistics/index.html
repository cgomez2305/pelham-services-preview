Navigating Challenges in U.S. Logistics: How Pelham Services Delivers Stability

September 28, 2025 · By Pelham Services

Running freight in the United States right now means operating on thin margins while absorbing shocks that are largely out of any one carrier's control — fuel prices that move week to week, a driver pool that hasn't kept pace with freight volume, regulatory requirements that shift, and customers who expect more transparency than the industry has traditionally offered. None of that is unique to us. What matters is how a carrier is built to absorb it.

Fuel price volatility

Diesel prices swing, and a lot of carriers pass that swing along to customers as a vague line-item that's hard to question. Our answer isn't to pretend the volatility doesn't exist — it's to be plain about how and when surcharges move, so the number a client sees on an invoice is one they were already told to expect. If you want a current rate on a specific lane, the fastest way to get a straight answer is to request a quote directly rather than work off a stale published rate card.

Driver and capacity shortages

The industry-wide driver shortage has pushed a lot of freight onto the spot market, where capacity and pricing can change overnight. We manage around that by running our own fleet rather than depending purely on brokered capacity. Owning the trucks and the drivers on our core California–Florida lane means we're not left scrambling for space when the broader market tightens — the capacity we've committed to a client is capacity we actually control.

Regulatory and compliance shifts

Rules around hours of service, emissions, and cross-border documentation don't stay still, and a loose network of independent owner-operators can be slow to adjust when they do. Because we operate as a directly-operated carrier with our own terminals in Gardena, CA and Miami, FL, compliance changes get implemented across one operation instead of negotiated across a patchwork of subcontractors. That's a structural advantage, not a marketing line — it's simply easier to move fast on a rule change when you're not waiting on someone else's fleet to catch up.

What this adds up to

None of these pressures are going away, and we're not claiming to be immune to them. What we can offer is a carrier that's built with fewer moving parts outside our own control — our own trucks, our own terminals, and pricing practices that don't hide the ball. That's what stability looks like in a market this unpredictable.
